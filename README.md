## Traditional AI vs Generative AI

### Traditional AI

Traditional AI mainly works with existing data to:

- Predict
- Detect
- Classify
- Optimise
- Recommend

**Aviation examples:**
- Predicting passenger demand
- Predicting flight delays
- Detecting unusual booking patterns
- Recommending fuel-efficient routes

### Generative AI

Generative AI mainly creates new content such as:

- Text
- Images
- Summaries
- Drafts
- Personalised communication

**Aviation examples:**
- Writing passenger delay messages
- Creating airline advertisements
- Preparing management summaries
- Creating promotional images

## Aviation Case Study

### Airline Customer Service

An airline can use Traditional AI to predict which flights are likely to be delayed.

Generative AI can then create personalised passenger notifications based on verified information.

Human employees should verify the information and approve the final communication before it is sent.

---

## Human Judgement

High-stakes aviation decisions should remain under authorised human control.

Examples include:

- Aircraft airworthiness decisions
- Aviation emergency decisions
- Serious safety-investigation decisions

AI should assist professionals rather than completely replace human judgement.

---

## AI Hallucination

A major risk of Generative AI is that it may produce information that sounds correct but is not verified.

In aviation, information such as:

- Compensation
- Hotel entitlement
- Meal entitlement
- Refund timelines
- Passenger rights
- Airline policies

should be checked against official and reliable sources.

---

## Key Learning

This lab demonstrated that Traditional AI is mainly used for prediction, detection and optimisation, while Generative AI is used to create new content.

The lab also showed that AI outputs should be verified by humans, especially when aviation safety, regulations or important passenger decisions are involved.

---

## Conclusion

Traditional AI and Generative AI can work together to improve aviation operations and passenger communication. However, responsible use of AI requires human verification, reliable information and appropriate human judgement for high-stakes decisions.
